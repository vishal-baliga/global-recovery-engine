# Global Recovery Engine — Product Offering & Competitive Strategy

**Updated:** September 7, 2026

**Status:** Current offering design and comparative hypotheses. Vendor descriptions below were checked on official pages on this date; GRE performance, provider acceptance, and availability remain unproved.

**Purpose:** Own product composition, capability choices, differentiation, and strategic expansion logic. Business/buyer decisions belong in [01](01-business-project-overview.md), committed scope in [06](06-pilot-brief.md), and comparative proof methods in [14](14-market-validation-and-proof.md).

## 1. Selected product and competitive job

The **Pilot combines GRE Foundation with GRE Trade & Receivables**, including the horizontal Network & Control capabilities that Trade needs. The initial corridor hypothesis is India-creditor → U.S.-business-obligor goods/services claims. Private Resolution, Consumer Credit Infrastructure, and standalone Network packaging are future candidates recorded in [07](07-future-development-and-business-deliverables.md).

GRE targets the work between a creditor's source systems and independently appointed providers: reconstruct the claim, establish the current financial/rights/authority position, assess route prerequisites, preflight against a named provider, obtain an explicit response, and retain a usable record through corrections and provider change.

The proposed advantage is **less work and fewer critical errors from source evidence to an accepted or correctly declined placement, with continuity after handoff**. It is a comparative target. A graph, document summary, AI agent, dashboard, or published schema does not establish it.

The buyer must value creditor ownership of the provider relationship. A recurring creditor with fragmented files and a provider panel is a stronger hypothesis than a one-off business asking someone to collect one invoice. Direct appointment adds visible responsibility; GRE must repay that friction through preparation, acceptance, transparency, and continuity value.

## 2. Composition and product packages

A supported offering composes Foundation, a matter family profile, a directional corridor, permitted service modes, and an identified executor/authority pattern. Commercial entitlements describe what was bought. Execution mode describes how a specific action may be performed. Neither substitutes for current policy and release eligibility.

| Pilot package | Customer outcome | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Claim Readiness** | A source-linked, versioned Claim Passport with parties, performance, balance, rights, authority, disputes, contradictions, and missing evidence | Readiness is for a stated purpose; integrity is not proof of truth or enforceability. |
| **Route & Economics** | Explainable options, prerequisites, cost/time scenarios, review needs, and a useful stop/no-route result | Separate evidence sufficiency, route availability, practical collectability, and net-value assumptions. No uncalibrated recovery probability. |
| **Provider Preflight & Transfer** | A named profile comparison, minimized disclosure, structured validation delta, exact offer, direct appointment, and provider response | Review disclosure, conditional/rejected/accepted placement, mandate, fulfilled conditions, and activation are separate. |
| **Creditor Continuity** | Scoped status/cost/payment observations, corrections, exceptions, export, recall, and switching continuity | GRE transports decisions and records effects; the independent provider conducts collection. |

These packages are parts of one Pilot. Assessment can finish successfully without placement. Transfer can finish while an independently scoped observation service continues. The obligation, provider engagement, and service completion do not share one misleading “done” state.

The primary surfaces are a creditor workspace, a GRE review/support workspace, and a minimal provider review/return surface or controlled adapter. They share the same records and checks. Provider-operated obligor communications and negotiation remain outside GRE's collection operations. A GRE correction/support channel must route received issues to their accountable owner without becoming a negotiation desk. A generalized debtor portal, outbound collection engine, white-label estate, and public integration product are not Pilot commitments.

## 4. Six reusable product contracts

The six named contracts organize reuse and external understanding. They compose existing canonical records where possible, with exact ownership and versioning in document 05; they do not create six independently editable copies of case truth.

| Contract | Product purpose | Proof of usefulness |
|---|---|---|
| **Claim Passport** | Purpose-scoped view of evidence, obligation, uncertainty, rights, authority, disputes, and disclosure lineage | Another organization reuses the record with a visible, limited validation delta. |
| **Provider Acceptance Profile** | Provider-authored or explicitly approved requirements, exclusions, credentials, formats, capacity, fee assumptions, and return expectations | Preflight predicts the provider's actual intake requirements; GRE cannot silently change them. |
| **Authority Envelope** | Exact principal, executor, acts, limits, expiry, revocation, and decision references | Appointment and subsequent instructions remain correctly bounded after change. |
| **Corridor Pack** | Direction, family, actors, activities, data/payment paths, effective policy, unresolved review, and release conditions | A rule/profile change identifies affected work and produces reviewable regression evidence. |
| **Action Receipt** | Attribution of proposal, approval, transmission, response, observation, failure, uncertainty, and correction | Users can tell what GRE did and what a provider merely reported. |
| **Resolution Quality Scorecard** | Separate readiness, effort/time, net economics, treatment, durability, and continuity observations | The buyer can assess value without hiding disputes, missing data, reversals, or service cost. |

## 6. Differentiation and evidence ladder

The prospective advantage joins evidence, acceptance, authority, policy, correction, and outcome continuity. It becomes defensible through maintained provider mappings, qualified corridor upkeep, trusted operations, recurring integrations, permissioned evaluation cases, and measured reductions in rework. Open syntax and capable models may make the first implementation easier to copy; adoption and execution quality must carry the business.

A first diagnostic needs no network-size claim. One independent provider can prove one accepted placement. Two independent providers are needed before claiming bounded portability across them, including a demonstrated recall/replacement path and declared provider-specific extensions. “No re-intake,” global coverage, legal compliance, recovery lift, or market leadership require their own evidence and cannot follow from schema publication.

Start with bilateral acceptance. Consider a minimal public Passport core and conformance kit after independent reuse supports it. Keep privacy-conscious extensions and version/deprecation rules. Reconsider broader standards governance only when other organizations contribute implementation and maintenance; publishing a format does not create an industry standard.

Use the scorecard and claim-evidence rules in [14](14-market-validation-and-proof.md), with activation controlled by [13](13-pilot-release-and-assurance.md). Repeated paid cohorts should establish lower total preparation/acceptance effort and critical errors while preserving correction and treatment quality. Return-data coverage, observation horizon, case mix, and selection bias limit economic conclusions.

## 7. Expansion and technical restraint

Private Resolution preserves a future participant-controlled clarification and voluntary agreement model, including useful private assessment if the other person never joins. Consumer Credit preserves institutional account and cross-border mobility needs, including disputes, preferences, protection holds, creditor oversight, and reviewed data topology. Standalone Network preserves broader panel administration, embedded distribution, and cross-provider assurance. Each needs its own buyer, terms, support, release evidence, and economics; shared Foundation code cannot enable it by implication.

The longer-term opportunities include source-connected readiness, better route economics, portfolio assurance, and bounded agent interaction. These reopen through the retained F-series entries, not a forecast that all will ship. A tenant-private deployment must remain valuable without pooled data rights.

Build one modular application using the selected [architecture](08-stack-and-environment-decision.md). A graph-shaped domain needs no graph database; durable workflow needs no distributed event-sourcing rewrite; product contracts need no service-per-object deployment. Preserve evaluated adapters and source ownership while deferring speculative channel breadth, proprietary models, blockchain, agent-per-object designs, and premature infrastructure changes. The sole-developer constraint determines implementation order and automation depth without shrinking essential correctness, support, or complete Pilot outcomes.
