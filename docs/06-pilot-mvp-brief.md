# Global Recovery Engine — Pilot MVP Brief

**Date:** September 6, 2026  
**Version:** 0.1 — proposed pilot scope for review  
**Owner:** Vishal, project lead and initial developer  
**Purpose:** Define a substantial, usable pilot before selecting the stack/environment and prioritizing delivery.

## 1. Product promise and pilot purpose

**Help a creditor turn an unresolved payment problem into an evidence-backed, actively managed path to resolution—from reconstructing what happened through borrower engagement, specialist assistance, payment tracking, and an explicit outcome.**

The pilot must serve real cases in both markets below. Useful outcomes include repayment, a workable agreement, resolution of a dispute, an accepted specialist handoff, or a reasoned decision to stop. Assessment and a signed plan are valuable milestones; neither means money was recovered.

GRE supplies the customer experience, intelligence, coordination, controls, and case records. A named, eligible creditor or provider performs the applicable contact and recovery work; qualified specialists perform reserved professional work. Each case has an accountable coordination owner. The actual operating arrangement must be established before that service goes live.

## 2. Two complete customer journeys

| Pilot track | Customer and problem | Required journey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Domestic U.S. personal/friend loans** | An individual lender has already struggled to recover money from another individual in the U.S., often without a written agreement. | Reconstruct advances, repayment understanding, prior contact, promises, and payments → review evidence and balance → select assistance → enable an appropriate borrower response → resolve, track a plan, obtain specialist help, or close with reasons. |
| **B. India–U.S. commercial recovery** | An Indian business, directly or through its recovery operator, has an unpaid commercial claim against a U.S. business. | Establish the correct parties, authority, contract/invoices, performance, disputes, and outstanding balance → choose a supported U.S. resolution route → coordinate creditor/provider work → reconcile receipts and cross-border costs → record settlement and remaining administrative duties. |

Track B retains the existing B2B use case. Here, “cross-border payments” means recovering an existing unpaid obligation. Cross-border personal loans and a general remittance service are outside this pilot.

Track A is an initial marketing and acquisition priority; Track B tests recurring business/operator value. Their demand and economics are measured separately. Delivery can proceed domestic first, then cross-border, while both remain pilot commitments.

Older, disputed, already-partly-paid, and previously handled matters need explicit intake paths. Missing documents or repayment dates remain unknown. The earlier B2B amount/age profile is a discovery hypothesis, not a universal admission rule. Supported states, claim profiles, languages, and service routes must be published before live acceptance; nationwide execution is not assumed.

## 3. Included pilot deliverables

These are required outcomes, not a prioritized backlog. Software, reviewed manual operations, and partner services may jointly deliver them; each manual step needs an owner, recorded result, and measured effort.

| Capability | What the pilot must deliver |
| --- | --- |
| **Acquisition and suitability** | Two clear customer entry journeys, service explanation, eligibility screening, fee/scope disclosure, inquiry/intake conversion tracking, and an honest outcome for unsupported submissions. |
| **Creditor onboarding and case intake** | Individual and organization accounts; identity/authority checks appropriate to the service; secure, resumable intake; duplicates review; customer objectives, budget, prior recovery attempts, existing representatives, agreements, and proceedings. Borrower enrollment is not required to begin assessment. |
| **Evidence reconstruction** | Upload documents, scans/photos, bank/payment records, relevant messages, emails, and supporting audio/transcripts. Preserve originals and distinguish recollections, translations, extracts, and reviewed findings. Produce a source-linked timeline, missing-evidence requests, contradictions, and an exportable case packet (“Claim Passport”). Manual review/translation may support the initial formats. |
| **Claim and balance assessment** | Separate a transfer from evidence of a loan, and an invoice from proof of performance. Reconcile multiple advances/invoices, alleged and supported repayments, credits, supported charges, and disputed portions. Present facts, gaps, practical options, indicative effort/cost, and uncertainty; supply no invented terms or recovery probabilities. |
| **Case workspace and coordination** | Creditor status/approvals, operator work queue, named next owner, deadlines/review dates, costs, activity history, and evidence requests. Import an existing plan or specialist matter at its verified current position. Show waiting, failed delivery, overdue response, reassignment, and service expiry explicitly. |
| **Supported contact and borrower response** | A working contact route using the identified authorized actor, approved content, and recorded delivery/replies. Provide secure claim viewing, evidence exchange, corrections, full/partial dispute, prior-payment reports, hardship explanation, and proposals. Viewing/responding must not require admitting the debt or paying GRE. Preserve non-portal participation through attributed records. |
| **Negotiation and agreements** | Proposals, counteroffers, full/partial settlements, relationship-sensitive options, and installment plans; clear costs, dates, conditions, and release terms. Record each party's authority and acceptance of the exact terms, including supported external acceptance. Silence and changed conditions are not automatic acceptance. |
| **Plan servicing and payments** | Supported hosted payment links or independently verified creditor payment instructions; funds use the established creditor/bank/provider arrangement. Track installments, permitted reminders, direct/off-platform payments, cash assertions, mismatches, duplicates, reversals, and renegotiation. Distinguish reported payment from source-verified receipt and allocation. For Track B, retain original currency, actual FX/fees, remittance evidence, and creditor net proceeds. |
| **Specialist assistance** | Eligible agency, mediation, counsel/small-claims, or other appropriate referral routes where supported. Provide the reviewed packet, quoted scope/cost, creditor authorization, provider acceptance, owner, status/deadline/cost updates, and return observations. Full court automation is unnecessary; an unaccepted referral is not a completed handoff. Provider decline or unavailability must return a clear alternative or disposition. |
| **Completion and customer records** | A downloadable outcome record showing original assertion, corrections/disputes, terms, observed receipts, remaining balance, costs, and reasons for closure/transfer. Track unresolved legal, bank/export, monitoring, and retention duties with owners. Payment or authority changes must stop/revise affected pending actions and allow reviewed reopening. |
| **Trust, support, and operating reliability** | Private case access, controlled disclosure/redaction, secure storage, auditable material changes, retention/deletion handling, and backup/recovery. Establish complaint, wrong-person, coercion/abuse, urgent-review, and incident paths. Protect confidential negotiation limits. AI may assist extraction, summaries, options, and drafts; authority, money, and external actions require independently enforced checks. |

The personal-loan path must handle acknowledged debt, “it was a gift,” disputed amount, claimed cash repayment, hardship, missing terms, little evidence, and an unreachable borrower. Transfer recipient and alleged borrower may differ. Support useful assessment without certifying an unsupported claim.

The commercial path must handle entity mismatch, disputed performance, partial payment, prior settlement/provider involvement, and cross-border receipt differences. Serious legal/deadline, insolvency, identity, or eligibility issues receive scoped specialist review while other permitted work can continue.

For both tracks, missing evidence, debtor refusal, or uneconomic recovery can lead to an explicit unresolved/closed outcome. No recovery guarantee, public shaming, fabricated charge, or unsupported escalation belongs in the service.

## 4. Business and launch work included in the pilot

These are pilot dependencies, even where implementation details remain open:

- **Service responsibility and coverage:** Decide the contracting structure, GRE's actual role, contact actor, qualified review ownership, supported states/actions, and cross-border payment/administrative arrangements. Review applicable requirements for the specific service before live data use, contact, payment activity, or paid operation as relevant.
- **A deliverable commercial offer:** Define assessment, assisted resolution, specialist coordination, and plan-servicing scope, service windows, fees/caps, cancellation/refund treatment, and outside costs. Proposed pricing direction is defined service work for individuals and case/capacity-based work for business/operators; exact prices remain open. Recovery-contingent pricing remains a future candidate.
- **Execution and support capacity:** Establish real partner coverage, accepted responsibilities, secure information exchange, response expectations, complaint handling, and a fallback when a provider cannot act. Someone must actually own each manual obligation.
- **Pilot recruitment and measurement:** Prepare track-specific messaging and onboarding; recruit permissioned cases and suitable participants when authorized. Record channel, conversion, willingness to pay, actual costs, feedback, and repeat/referral intent. Start with invited cohorts sized to support capacity; set cohort size and service targets before launch.

Technical and contractual details become research/implementation work before the features that depend on them. They need not all be settled before selecting the stack or beginning development.

## 5. What will establish pilot readiness and value

| Question | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Can both journeys operate end to end? | Demonstrated intake, review, authorized contact, response/dispute, agreement, payment/exception handling, specialist handoff, and closure; permissioned real cases in both tracks. No required step may depend on an unnamed person or unavailable service. |
| Does it survive realistic failures? | Verify wrong-party/weak-evidence cases, changed or revoked authority, private-data separation, a stale demand after payment, provider decline, uncertain delivery, duplicate/reversed payments, and missed installments. Unresolved critical failures block the affected live capability. |
| Does it help customers? | Time to useful assessment/next action, total customer and operator effort, accepted handoffs and rework, borrower participation, dispute outcomes, actual receipts/net proceeds, plan performance, complaints, and unresolved cases. |
| Can it support a business? | Track acquisition/conversion, paid uptake, service revenue, founder/reviewer/support time, technology/data and partner costs, contribution per case, and repeat/referral evidence separately for A and B. |

Set numerical service/quality/economic targets against observed baselines before evaluating the live cohort. Synthetic cases establish behavior, not demand or recovery results. Report sample size, observation window, missing outcomes, and open plans; attribute historical payments separately. A payment plan still in progress cannot be reported as completed recovery.
