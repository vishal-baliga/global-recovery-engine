## 1. Why Trade is first

Trade & Receivables best reuses GRE's existing evidence, financial-position, authority and provider-continuity design. Recurring preparation and provider intake work creates a plausible repeat budget. The verified stack is capable of supporting the other families too; it does not establish their customers, safety model or institutional readiness.

| Candidate | Reason for current disposition | Reopening evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Trade & Receivables | Selected Pilot: recurring complex commercial files and a bounded preparation-to-provider job can be tested with the existing design | Continue only if reachable paid demand, provider participation and support-adjusted economics hold |
| Private Resolution | Future: preparation-only value and voluntary counterpart participation remain uncertain; separate privacy/safety/support work is substantial | Credible direct/sponsor distribution, evidence of useful preparation and voluntary participation, reviewed activity and viable support cost |
| Consumer Credit | Future: meaningful recurring potential but institutional integration, account continuity, complaint/protection, procurement and data-role dependencies are largest | A regulated design partner, actual account/corridor need, qualified legal/data review and institutional assurance capacity |
| Network & Control | Trade-required capabilities included now; standalone horizontal product is future | More than one paying use case and independent provider adoption beyond a bespoke Trade implementation |

Do not assume a channel is reachable because its logo would be valuable. Start with a creditor the founder can actually access, with a named operations/budget owner and repeated eligible files. An insurer, factor, servicer or embedded AR channel is attractive when it has those properties, but is not a prerequisite for the first diagnostic.

Established providers already offer convenient online invoice submission, status tracking and contingency collection. GRE must show value beyond upload and a dashboard. [Atradius online collection](https://atradiuscollections.com/global/services/online-debt-collection). The narrower hypothesis is reduced total preparation/re-intake work, fewer consequential corrections and preserved context across independently accountable providers. Vendor claims are competitive context, not independent performance evidence.

## 2. Discovery hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Evidence to collect | Evidence that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| A reachable creditor has recurring complex Trade files | Eligible annual/monthly volume, source formats, case ages/values, current internal owner and existing provider route | Mostly one-off, clean or very low-value invoices; no budget/operations owner |
| Preparation/re-intake is an expensive job | Actual time samples, missing-document loops, correction types, provider review and integration effort | Collection execution dominates cost and better preparation changes little |
| A provider accepts a reusable core | Declared evidence/data/authority requirements, validation delta, direct acceptance and return-event agreement | Complete duplicate intake required regardless of Passport quality |
| The hard handoff is acceptable | Customer understanding of separate direct engagement, transparent fees, oversight and residual duties | Buyers overwhelmingly demand a one-stop operator and will not pay for continuity |
| The corridor has sufficient accessible volume | Named India creditors with U.S. commercial claims and a supported provider/state/activity profile | General trade statistics without reachable unpaid-case volume |
| GRE can deliver profitably | Willingness to pay independent of recovery, founder/reviewer/support minutes, software/data costs and incident effort | Manual effort consumes the fee or every customer requires a bespoke product |

Discovery notes separate what a person said, observed records and the founder's inference. Record sample selection, role, permission, date, evidence confidence and follow-up. Interviews alone do not establish price acceptance or production use. Do not upload real customer information to Git.

## 3. Evidence sequence

1. Identify a reachable recurring creditor; document the current workflow and exact eligible claim profile. Identify a cooperating provider when named-provider preflight or placement is in scope. Creditor-only readiness and no-viable-route assessment remain valid services without one.
2. Agree a fixed-scope historical Readiness Diagnostic with a concrete deliverable, permitted data, price/cost hypothesis and useful negative outcomes. Obtain the required data/security/activity readiness first.
3. Pre-register comparison with the customer's current practice and, where useful for testing differentiation, a capable general model plus ordinary workflow tools. Keep inputs, permissions, scope and total human effort comparable; protect real evidence under the same data rules. Use a small varied cohort the customer can actually supply; 30–50 historical files from the research directive is a candidate sample ambition, not a compulsory first batch or a statistical guarantee.
4. Produce source-linked readiness and, when a named provider/profile is available and in scope, provider-specific preflight; measure creditor, provider and GRE labor including manual intervention and integration cost. Capture rejected and insufficient-evidence files.
5. After the separate prospective release gate, complete one real direct placement with a current mandate, independent acceptance, defined event return and accountable continuity work.
6. Demonstrate repeat paid use. Add a second independent provider and prove bounded reuse/recall/switch before advertising portability across providers.

The first diagnostic does not require all providers or every planned interface. It must deliver the actual contracted job and comply with its applicable controls. Synthetic tests prove modeled behavior; historical files test reconstruction; prospective work tests actual execution seams. Keep those evidence levels separate.

## 4. Measurement contract

Measure total work and customer value. Use the [Quality Scorecard contract](05-domain-data-contracts.md) with explicit provenance, denominator, observation window and missingness.

| Dimension | Minimum Pilot measurements |
|---|---|
| Demand and economics | Eligible leads/files, conversions, paid diagnostic/recurring acceptance, actual service revenue, founder/reviewer/support effort, data/compute/integration cost and repeat use |
| Readiness | Preparation time, critical party/balance/rights/authority/evidence errors, missingness, corrections and material unresolved contradictions |
| Provider acceptance | Time to explicit decision, first-pass/conditional/rejected/expired results, delta size and provider review effort |
| Continuity | Event coverage/freshness, correction delivery/acknowledgment, no-response escalation, export completeness, recall and replacement activation evidence |
| Financial outcomes | Source-supported payments, fees/FX and reversals; residual balance and outstanding duties. Separate GRE service value from external collection results |
| Safety/quality | Unauthorized disclosure/actions, complaints, wrong-party or destination errors, incident impact and reopened disputes |
| AI contribution | Raw output errors, final reviewed accuracy, abstention/refusal, reviewer time, harmful-error cases, latency and cost |

Keep nonresponding, rejected, unresolved, withdrawn and still-open cases in the appropriate denominators. Retain observed cash, cost and status through the measurement horizon. Mark an unfinished time-to-event outcome as right-censored; distinguish it from a missing or unobserved record. A provider-reported settlement, a promise or a workflow completion is not recovered money. Do not attribute causal recovery uplift to GRE from selected or incomparable cases.

Before prospective evaluation, negotiate numerical success and stop thresholds with the design partner. Record the baseline, cohort/sample rule, definition, source, horizon and business action if a threshold is met or missed. A one-third manual-effort reduction from the earlier research is a discussion target, not an adopted promise. A statistically unsupported metric cannot become marketing copy merely because it is favorable.

## 5. Public claims and launch posture

| Evidence actually held | Permitted bounded claim |
|---|---|
| Designed or synthetic | Describe designed capability and exact demonstration conditions |
| Historical diagnostic | Report authorized dataset/profile and measured preparation/review results with limitations |
| One live provider | Describe the exact released service and actual accepted placement results; no universal network or switching claim |
| Two independent accepting providers | Describe portability for those supported profiles and measured additional intake/switch conditions |
| Repeat comparable paid cohorts | Publish scoped improvements with method, dates, denominator, missingness and permission |

Market leadership is an objective. A country count, AI label, standard schema or inaccessible competitor feature does not prove it. Name a customer or provider publicly only with permission; publish redacted examples only after disclosure review. The control plane should remain useful even if pooled benchmarking and model training are unavailable.

## 6. Stop, narrow or revisit

If the corridor lacks reachable eligible volume, change the supported Trade profile only through an explicit scope/release decision. If preparation produces little total-work saving, narrow to the highest-friction claim subtype or stop expanding. If providers will not accept a common core, test provider-side preparation value instead of claiming a network. If customers require managed collection, record that demand as a separate business-model review; do not simulate a principal collector through interface labels.

No automatic switch to Private or Consumer occurs because Trade demand fails. Reopen those [future candidates](07-future-development-and-business-deliverables.md) with their own evidence, operating model and scope decision.
